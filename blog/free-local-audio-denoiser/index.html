Free local audio denoiser: clean voice recordings without uploading

By Bronze · October 1, 2026 · 4 min read

CleanTake removes steady room noise — fan, air-conditioner hum — from recorded voice files, entirely on your computer. No upload, no account, no subscription. On a real 25-second recording tested September 28, 2026, it cut environmental noise by about 32 dB while the voice stayed intact. It cannot separate overlapping voices, and since October 2, 2026, there’s a free public download — a portable ZIP or one-click installer for Windows.

What kind of noise does it remove — and what can't it do?

It handles

  • Steady room noise: fans, air-conditioners, electrical hum
  • Hiss from cheap microphones and interfaces
  • Voice recordings where the speaker is the only voice

It can't do (yet)

  • Overlapping voices. In a café-style test with four people talking over each other, it reduced everything evenly — voice included. It does not separate one voice from a crowd.
  • Real-time calls. It processes recorded files, not live audio. It is not a Krisp replacement for Zoom.
  • Music. It is tuned for speech; singing and instruments are not its job.

How was this tested? Exact method, versions, and numbers

No cherry-picking claims without a method. Here is everything needed to reproduce the result:

  • Source: a real 25.0-second voice recording in a room with fan/air-con hum (MP3, 400 KB, 48 kHz mono). Not synthesized, not re-recorded.
  • Pipeline: ffmpeg → DeepFilterNet 0.5.6 → loudness normalize to −16 LUFS (I=−16, TP=−1.5, LRA=11). Output: MP3, 601 KB.
  • Result: environmental noise reduced by ≈32 dB, voice preserved — measured on this sample, 2026-09-28. Your mileage varies with the room.
  • Speed: the full pipeline processed this 25-second clip in about 5 seconds on a standard CPU with no GPU (timed 2026-09-30, Linux test machine).
  • Re-check 2026-09-30: an independent measurement of the noise floor in two speech pauses (ffmpeg volumedetect) showed the floor dropping from −33.0 dB to −51.1/−59.3 dB — an 18–26 dB reduction under a different, stricter metric. The ≈32 dB figure is the original full-sample measurement; both are published so you can see the method matters.
  • Voice preservation: a listening assessment (the voice sounds the same), not a lab metric — labeled as such.

The pipeline is the same one documented on how CleanTake works, and the failure cases are on the limits page.

Who is this for — and who should skip it?

  • Podcasters and voiceover folks recording at home with untreated rooms — fan and AC noise is the single most common complaint.
  • Anyone shipping voice notes, course audio, or narration who wants a free local option without uploading client audio to a server.
  • Skip it if your audio has multiple people talking over each other, or you need real-time noise removal on live calls.
