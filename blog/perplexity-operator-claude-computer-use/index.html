Perplexity Computer vs OpenAI Operator vs Claude Computer Use: three philosophies, one dead

By Bronze · October 24, 2026 · 6 min read

Everyone wants an AI that uses your computer for you. But the three biggest attempts at it are not really competitors — they are three different philosophies. One sells you the result. One sells you the capability. And one is already dead, which makes it the most instructive of the three.

1. Perplexity Computer — selling the result

Perplexity Computer is a "digital worker": give it a goal, it orchestrates roughly 20 frontier models (reasoning core is Claude Opus 4.6; sub-agents use Gemini for deep research, Nano Banana for images, Veo 3.1 for video, Grok for light tasks, ChatGPT 5.2 for long context — all per Perplexity's own announcement), splits the task into subtasks, and runs the workflow asynchronously for hours, days, even months. It runs inside Perplexity's isolated cloud environment with a real filesystem, a real browser, and 400+ integrations (Slack, Gmail, GitHub).

It launched in late February 2026 and is still alive. There are variants: Personal Computer (runs locally on Mac/Mac mini, rolling out since May 2026, fully-local from October 2026), Portable Computer (local on Nvidia hardware, August 2026), and Enterprise editions.

The catch is the price and the billing model. Perplexity Computer ships with the Perplexity Max plan at $200/month (per Perplexity's pricing page, corroborated by press coverage in October 2026). The $20/month Pro plan only gets a one-time 4,000-credit trial. And because agents retry when connectors fail, credits can burn fast — with no reliable cost estimate shown before you run, according to hands-on reviews.

Philosophy: don't build anything, rent the outcome. No setup, no code, no servers. But you rent the whole agent, its environment, and its roadmap — which means you can be priced out, throttled, or re-planned at the vendor's discretion.

2. OpenAI Operator — the cautionary tale

OpenAI Operator was the browser-automation agent for consumers: it looked at screenshots, reasoned, and emitted clicks and keystrokes, running on OpenAI's cloud browser (the CUA model — GPT-4o plus reinforcement learning). It filled in forms, placed orders, booked appointments, like a real user.

It launched January 23, 2025 as a research preview for ChatGPT Pro users in the US. Then the timeline went like this: folded into the ChatGPT agent on July 17, 2025; the standalone app shut down at the end of August 2025; the successor ChatGPT agent itself was removed from ChatGPT in early August 2026 (OpenAI's Help Center now says "ChatGPT agent is no longer available", pointing to ChatGPT Work); the Atlas browser also sunset on August 9, 2026. The capability survives only in fragments: a cloud browser here, a Chrome extension there, an app somewhere else.

When alive, Operator required the ChatGPT Pro plan at $200/month — the only tier that had it during research preview.

Lifecycle: ~19 months from launch to the successor being removed. That's the lesson, and it's the whole point of this section: anyone who built automation workflows on top of Operator woke up to a platform that no longer exists. Not deprecated — gone.

3. Claude's Computer Use — selling the capability

Anthropic went the opposite direction. Computer Use is not an app — it's a computer tool on the Claude API. You build the agent. You provide the environment (Anthropic recommends a dedicated VM or container). The loop is simple: screenshot → the model looks at the image, computes pixel coordinates (x, y) → returns tool calls (mouse/keyboard) → your app executes them → screenshot again to verify → repeat.

It was announced October 22, 2024 alongside Claude 3.5 Sonnet as an "experimental" beta that Anthropic itself called cumbersome and error-prone, and reached general availability in August 2026 with toolset computer_toolset_20260801 (no beta header needed, batch actions, default zoom). The recommended model is Claude Sonnet 4.5 (released September 29, 2025), which Anthropic calls its best computer-using model, scoring 61.4% on OSWorld. Official API pricing: $3 input / $15 output per 1M tokens.

Philosophy: no product, just capability. You get the raw power and the raw bill. You also get the raw responsibility: the harness, the VM, the guardrails, the prompt-injection defenses (Anthropic's own docs require isolated environments, least-privilege, domain allowlists, and human confirmation before actions with real consequences — because a screenshot is an attack surface).

What builders should actually take from this

The three products are a spectrum, and your position on it is a business decision, not a technical one:

  1. Rent the outcome (Perplexity): fastest to value, but your unit economics and your roadmap belong to someone else. Fine for prototyping and non-core workflows. Dangerous as load-bearing infrastructure.
  2. Buy the capability (Anthropic): you own the agent, the environment, and the exit. The cost is real engineering work — sandboxing, guardrails, harness. The bill is proportional to usage, not to a tier.
  3. Remember the dead one (OpenAI): 19 months, end to end. Any automation you build on a vendor agent needs an abstraction layer and an exit plan — a documented way to move the same workflow to another provider or your own infra. If you can't describe your exit in two sentences, you don't have one.

There's a fourth option the comparison table hides: run it on your own machine. Local-first agents (open models + your own harness) trade capability for ownership — the same trade this blog has been tracking since the $50 hack. The vendors will keep racing on capability. Ownership is the one axis where you don't have to wait for them.
